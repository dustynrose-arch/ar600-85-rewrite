# AR 600–85 Revision — User Guide

**Internal G–1 rewrite working group use only.**  
This application never changes the original regulation (read-only).

---

## 0. Purpose

This tool helps the G–1 rewrite working group draft **AR 600–85** without touching the official regulation.

Everything you write here is a **draft**. It is not Army policy and not an authenticated publication until official release. Never treat seals, Word stamps, or a password-locked site as final. The plain gold **DRAFT** mark in the header (or **TRAINING / DRAFT** in Training) is intentional.

**Original regulation (read-only):** AR 600–85 (4 Oct 2024; administrative revisions 27 Feb 2025 / 19 Feb 2026).

**Who this Guide is for:** Experienced ASAP professionals on the rewrite working group — Editors, Reviewers, and Approvers.

**How to use this Guide:** Work by job. Start with access and layout if you are new; jump to compare, Training, or export when you already know the panes.

## Working-group access (password gate)

**What it is**  
The live rewrite site is password-locked for the working group. It is a draft tool, not an official Army publication and not open to the public.

**When to use it**  
First visit, after **Log out**, after you close the browser, or in a private/incognito window.

**How**
1. Open the private link you were given.
2. **Working-group access** asks for the password on a first visit, after **Log out**, and again after you close the browser. Enter the password, then continue into the app.
3. Keep the password and the site link inside the working group. Do not forward the link widely or paste passwords, storage tokens, or other secrets into chat, email, or screenshots.

**What success looks like**  
After you close the browser, click **Log out**, or open a private window, the login page comes first. The draft app appears after the correct password. Seals appear with the plain gold DRAFT mark.

**Session reminder**  
The login lasts only while this browser stays open. Closing the browser clears it, so the next visit asks for the password again. Click **Log out** in the header to end the session immediately and return to **Working-group access**. Refreshing or opening another tab in the same open browser stays signed in until you log out or quit the browser.

**Common mistake**  
Wrong password keeps you on the login page — try again; do not paste the password into team chat. Edits on this site stay on the password-locked draft. They are not published APD / public domain until official release.

## Part A — Orient the workspace

### 1. Top banner & chrome

**What it is**  
The dark top bar holds the **G-1 seal** on the left, then the left-aligned title **AR 600-85 Revision** and subtitle **Directorate of Prevention, Resilience and Readiness**, then the **Army seal** (seals flanking that text). A plain gold **DRAFT** (or **TRAINING / DRAFT**) mark is centered on the full banner. Training controls, Role, the three gold export buttons, **User Guide**, and **Log out** follow on the same bar. Seals are always paired with the DRAFT mark — never shown alone as if this were an authenticated publication.

**When to use it**  
Any time you need to know live vs Training, change role, export, or open this Guide.

**How**
1. Confirm the G-1 seal, left-aligned title and subtitle, and Army seal form a left sandwich, and the plain gold DRAFT mark is centered on the full banner (never seals alone).
2. Read live (`DRAFT`) vs Training (`TRAINING / DRAFT`). Header plain **DRAFT** is not the same string as Word’s **DRAFT / WORKING COPY** stamp — both are intentional.
3. **Training controls** (separate from export): **Enter Training** / **Leave Training** (cream-on-dark) / **Reset to original**.
4. **Gold export buttons:** **Plain** · **Track Changes** · **Summary**, plus **User Guide** (opens over this draft; the draft beside it stays on screen) and **Log out**.
5. Set **Role** (`Editor` / `Reviewer` / `Approver`) before gated actions.
6. **Log out** (same gold header button) clears the working-group session and returns to the password page. Closing the browser does the same.

**What success looks like**  
You can name seals, DRAFT mark, Training controls, and the three export buttons without hunting. The header is the dark theme (black bar, cream text) — there is no separate theme toggle.

**Common mistake**  
Grouping **Leave Training** with the gold export buttons (Leave is cream-on-dark). Treating DRAFT on a Word file as an error, or looking for save status in this header row (save status lives under center **Your draft**).

### 2. Three panes (layout)

**What it is**  
Three panes: **Outline** on the left; stacked original + draft in the center; **Writing Assistant** on the right.

**When to use it**  
Every session — find text, write, and check reminders.

**How**
1. **Left — Outline:** Pick a chapter, appendix, or Glossary entry. A **rust ●** means that section already differs from the original regulation.
2. **Center:** Read **ORIGINAL REGULATION — READ ONLY** on top. Edit **Your draft** underneath. Always stacked — never side-by-side.
3. **Right — Writing Assistant:** Open when you need glossary, Limited Use, overlap, Versions/compare, Summary, Timeline, Authority, Tasks, or Upload help.

**What success looks like**  
You can point to left / center / right and say: find it · write it · check it. DRAFT is still visible in the header.

**Common mistake**  
Treating the whole center as one editable box. Only the lower **Your draft** band takes typing.

**Tiny tour:** Left = find it. Center = write it. Right = check it. Top = export it.

### 3. Hide / show panels

**What it is**  
Collapse **Outline** and/or **Writing Assistant** so the center editor widens. The center pane has no hide control.

**When to use it**  
Long drafting when you want more room; restore sides when you need search, structure tools, or reminders.

**How**
1. On the left pane, click **Hide outline**.
2. On the right pane, click **Hide Writing Assistant**.
3. To restore: **Show outline** / **Show Writing Assistant**, or the thin strip on that side.
4. Choices stick for this browser tab session.

**What success looks like**  
A wider draft band; either side restores in one click.

**Common mistake**  
Looking for hide controls on the center pane — they are not there.

### 4. Search & open a section

**What it is**  
Outline search finds wording in the **original regulation** (read-only), then opens that section in the center.

**When to use it**  
When you know a term from the official text and need to land on that paragraph quickly.

**How**
1. Use **Search original regulation** (placeholder **Search AR 600-85**).
2. Open a hit — or click any chapter / section in the list.
3. Confirm center shows original above and Your draft below.

**Tips**
- A **rust ●** means that section already differs from the original regulation (not a gold dot).
- A **WG** mark means an Approver marked it ready for working-group review.
- **Summary of Change** at the top of the outline (with a DRAFT badge) opens the deltas list.

**What success looks like**  
The middle jumps to the section you chose; draft is ready underneath.

**Common mistake**  
Expecting search to scan your draft’s new wording. It searches the original regulation only.

## Part B — Edit your draft

### 5. Original regulation vs your draft (stacked)

**What it is**  
In the center, **ORIGINAL REGULATION — READ ONLY** sits above. **Your draft** sits below. Compare uses the same stack: original above, your draft below.

**When to use it**  
Every edit. Always write in the draft. Use the original only as reference.

**How**
1. Open a section from the left.
2. Place the cursor in **Your draft** (lower band).
3. Type your changes.
4. Leave the original regulation box alone.
5. If Compare is on, you may also see `COMPARISON: Original regulation (read-only)` or `COMPARISON: Saved checkpoint`.

**What success looks like**  
You can type only in the lower band. The upper text does not change. Header still shows DRAFT.

**Common mistake**  
Trying to type in the original. If you can’t type, you’re on the original — move to the draft underneath. Never call this layout side-by-side.

---

### 6. Undo & Save

**What it is**  
Editors (unlocked) can undo draft typing and force a manual save. Autosave still runs (~800 ms after you pause typing).

**When to use it**  
Undo after a bad phrase. **Save** when you want a clear manual-save line in the activity list.

**How**
1. Watch status **under the center Your draft heading**: `Last saved … · Saved` / `Saving…` / `Unsaved changes` (or `Read-only (role or lock)`).
2. Click **Undo**, or press Ctrl+Z / ⌘Z, to step back draft typing.
3. Click **Save** for a manual save now.
4. Reviewer / Approver / locked sessions: no Undo/Save buttons.

**What success looks like**  
Status shows **Saved**. Undo restored earlier draft wording. The original regulation never undoes.

**Common mistake**  
Looking for save status in the top header next to Role/export — it is under **Your draft**.

---

### 7. Spellcheck

**What it is**  
Your draft underlines likely misspellings in red. Right-click a marked word for the **SPELLING** menu. The original regulation is not spellchecked. There is no separate Spellcheck button in the chrome.

**When to use it**  
While drafting language where a typo would confuse the working group.

**How**
1. Type in Your draft.
2. Look for a red wavy underline.
3. Right-click the marked word → **SPELLING** (or **No suggestions**).
4. Pick a suggestion, or leave intentional ASAP vocabulary alone.

**What success looks like**  
The underline clears after you accept a fix, or you knowingly keep the word.

**Common mistake**  
Expecting spell marks on the original regulation, or hunting for a Spellcheck toolbar control.

---

### 8. Reshape the outline (Editors only)

**What it is**  
Editors can change the **structure of your draft** from Outline: add, split, delete, rename, reorder. The original regulation structure stays frozen.

**When to use it**  
When the working copy needs a new paragraph, a split, a title fix, or a move — not for a wording-only tweak.

**How**
1. Select a paragraph in Outline (or right-click it).
2. Choose Add before / after / paragraph, **Split**, **Rename**, or **Delete** (confirm delete).
3. Drag **::** to reorder within a chapter or onto another chapter.
4. Display numbers renumber automatically.

**What success looks like**  
The activity list records who, when, and the action. Writing Assistant reminders stay tied to that paragraph’s stable identity. Summary of Change shows Adds / Rescinds / Moves / Revises as appropriate.

**Common mistake**  
Deleting a draft paragraph and expecting the original regulation text to disappear.

Reviewers and Approvers can browse Outline but cannot reshape it.

## Part C — See what changed

### 9. Delta color (what changed)

**What it is**  
Color cues that help you spot change quickly. They are navigation aids — not legal clearance.

**When to use it**  
While editing (gold in the draft), scanning Outline (rust ●), or reviewing **Summary of Change** (full legend).

**How to read what ships today**
1. **In Your draft:** gold highlight on charcoal text marks **wording you added or changed** versus the original (or versus the compare body). Removals are **not** painted in the draft — that text is already gone.
2. **No legend beside the draft.** That is intentional. Open **Summary of Change** for the legend: *Gold marks wording added in your draft; rust strikethrough marks wording removed from the original.*
3. **Outline rust ●** — this section already differs from the original regulation (not a gold dot).
4. **Summary filters:** All / Revises / Adds / Rescinds / Moves.
5. **Document crossmatch** (separate job): Match (olive) / Miss (rust) / Unclear (gold).

**What success looks like**  
You see gold on new draft wording, then open Summary of Change when you need removals and the full Revises / Adds / Rescinds / Moves list.

**Common mistake**  
Looking for a color legend next to the draft; calling the outline marker a gold dot; reading a color as “approved.”

---

### 10. Compare (original above, your draft below)

**What it is**  
A stacked compare of the original regulation (or a checkpoint) against your draft, plus a simple list of wording changes and Summary of Change.

**When to use it**  
Before you share a section, before export, or when you need to prove what changed.

**How**
1. Open **Writing Assistant** → **Versions**.
2. Optional (Editors): name a checkpoint and save it.
3. Choose compare against **Original regulation (read-only)** or a saved checkpoint.
4. Click **Compare (original above, your draft below)**.
5. Read hunk headers: **Original regulation (above)** / **Your draft (below)**. Trace one real change top → bottom.
6. Click **List the changes** for wording bullets (not a legal review).
7. Open **Summary of Change** from Outline or the **Summary** tab for Revises / Adds / Rescinds / Moves.

**What success looks like**  
You see original on top and draft under for at least one changed paragraph, and the change matches your intent.

**Common mistake**  
Opening Versions and stopping before **Compare**, or calling the layout side-by-side.

## Part D — Writing Assistant (only when warranted)

### 11. Writing Assistant overview

**What it is**  
The right-hand **Writing Assistant** watches the paragraph you have open. It lists reminders when wording touches a locked glossary term, Limited Use / legal topics, or overlap with another publication. Reminders are **suggestions only** — Writing Assistant does **not** rewrite your draft unless you choose an action.

**When to use it**  
Only when a reminder appears and the topic is actually in play.

**How**
1. Open a paragraph in the center.
2. Open the **Writing Assistant** tab on the right.
3. Read **What this panel does**, then any sections that appear for this paragraph.
4. **Glossary — terms you must keep:** items may show as `Locked term: …` — that means keep the official ASAP wording (one plain meaning). Empty state: **No locked terms in this paragraph.**
5. If sister-pub buttons appear (`STEER AR 600-8-2`, `STEER AR 635-200`, `STEER AR 135-175`, `STEER AR 135-178`), treat them as a jump to **Authority** — not a rewrite. Prefer a short See AR … cite over pasting procedures.
6. Editors choose an action when offered: **Keep wording** · **Insert See cite** · **Open primary cite**.
7. Other tabs as needed: Authority · Tasks · Versions · Summary · Timeline · Upload. On **Tasks**, **Complete** marks an open task done. **Delete** removes that task from the draft immediately — it is not kept, listed, or recoverable.

**What success looks like**  
The right pane matches the paragraph you are on. Your draft does not change until you choose Keep wording or Insert See cite.

**Common mistake**  
Waiting for the tool to fix the paragraph for you. Reminders are suggestions only.

---

### 12. Limited Use Policy (self-referral) — when it shows vs hides

**What it is**  
Writing Assistant shows **two kinds** of legal help. Do not treat every legal reminder as Limited Use.

1. **Limited Use Policy (self-referral)** — AR 600–85 protection for qualifying self-referral cases only.
2. **Legal / adverse-action hint** — other rights / discipline risks (testing bases, process path, Art. 31 / coercion tone, civilian EAP–TDP, and similar). Empty state: **No legal / adverse-action hints in the current paragraph.**

The Limited Use Policy lives in paragraphs **10–11 through 10–13**. Writing Assistant cites **See para 10-12 / 10-13**. Canonical definition is **10–12**. FAQ pointer: appendix **B–10** — cite it; don’t paste it. Authority may also cite **AR 600-8-24** (officer separations) even if it is not on the STEER row. Header shows plain **DRAFT**; Word still uses **DRAFT / WORKING COPY**.

**When Limited Use appears**  
When the open paragraph is in the Limited Use–relevant set (including controlling paragraphs such as **10-11 through 10-13**, plus related ids the product locks — e.g. parts of ch. 7, 9-11, B-5, B-10) **or** your draft/title uses Limited Use / self-referral / self-identification / protected evidence wording.

**When Limited Use stays hidden**  
On Purpose, most admin chapters, and other unrelated chapters **unless** your draft wording trips those keywords. The Limited Use block can **appear and vanish as you move between paragraphs**. That is by design — not a broken Writing Assistant.

**How (cite-don’t-copy)**
1. When Limited Use shows: follow **See para 10-12 / 10-13**. Full policy span: paras **10–11 through 10–13**; FAQ pointer **B–10**. Cite sister pubs; do not copy their procedures into AR 600–85.
2. On-screen cite line: **See para 10-12 / 10-13. Suggestion only — Writing Assistant does not rewrite this paragraph.**
3. Use Authority / STEER jumps for sister publications (AR 600-8-2, 635-200, 135-175, 135-178). AR 600-8-24 may appear in Authority for officer separations — that is not a missing STEER bug.
4. Remember: Limited Use is **not** a shield for later misconduct or for refusing a lawful order to test.

**What success looks like**  
You treat Limited Use only when self-referral protection is actually in play. You do not “troubleshoot” Assist when Limited Use hides on Purpose or admin chapters.

**Common mistake**  
Reading every legal / adverse-action hint as Limited Use; or assuming Writing Assistant failed because Limited Use disappeared after you changed paragraphs.

### 13. Glossary and Authority

**What it is**  
Two ways the right pane keeps ASAP language consistent.

**When to use each**
- **Glossary — terms you must keep** — you used a defined term. Keep one official ASAP meaning. The `Locked term:` prefix means “keep this official wording.”
- **Authority** — topic belongs in a sister publication. Cite; do not paste procedures here. `STEER AR …` buttons open Authority; they do not rewrite your draft. Prefer a short See AR … cite.
- **Doctrine tip — overlap checks** — **Keep wording**, **Insert See cite**, or **Open primary cite**.

**What success looks like**  
One official meaning per locked term; short See cites instead of duplicated procedure text.

**Common mistake**  
Copying sister-pub procedures into AR 600–85 “for convenience,” or treating STEER as an auto-edit.

## Part E — Document crossmatch

### 14. Upload & compare a file to your draft

**What it is**  
Editors and Approvers upload a document and get suggestion-only compare rows against **your draft**. Suggestions never auto-write into your draft or the original regulation.

**When to use it**  
When someone sent a .docx, .pdf, or .pptx (≤ 25 MB) that should be checked against the working copy.

**How**
1. Open **Writing Assistant** → **Upload** → heading **Compare to your draft (25 MB)**.
2. Upload `.docx`, `.pdf`, or `.pptx`. Wait for **Comparing to your draft…** if shown.
3. Read **Suggestions — Match / Miss / Unclear**.
4. Row prefixes: **Your draft:** · **Upload:** · use **Open in your draft** to jump.
5. After more edits: **Compare again to your draft**.
6. **Miss** can flag unlocked ASAP wording (for example, “illegal drugs” → prefer locked “illicit use”; UDL where a locked term applies). Treat every row as a suggestion — decide yourself.
7. Reviewers see: **Reviewers cannot upload files. Switch to Editor or Approver.**
8. Approvers (same tab): **Approver WG-review** · **Mark this paragraph** · **Mark ready for WG review** · **Clear marks**.

**What success looks like**  
Suggestion rows appear (`No compare rows yet.` when empty); draft text did not change by itself; original regulation untouched.

**Common mistake**  
Expecting the upload to rewrite the draft automatically.

## Part F — Practice safely

### 15. Training mode + reset

**What it is**  
A separate practice copy of the same app. Live drafts, uploads, and activity stay untouched.

**When to use it**  
When you want to try outline moves, compare, or Writing Assistant actions without risking the live rewrite.

**How**
1. Click **Enter Training**.
2. Confirm banner **TRAINING** and header mark **TRAINING / DRAFT**. Banner body: practice copy does not change the live workspace.
3. Practice freely.
4. To wipe practice work: **Reset to original** (Editor or Approver) → **CONFIRM RESET** / **Reset the training copy?** → confirm. Reviewers see **Switch to Editor or Approver to reset**.
5. **Leave Training** (cream-on-dark Training control) to return to live (**DRAFT**).

**What success looks like**  
TRAINING marks while you practice. After reset, only the training copy is restored. Live workspace unchanged.

**Common mistake**  
Resetting Training and thinking you cleared the live rewrite.

## Part G — Hand off (export)

### 16–17. Export: Plain · Track Changes · Summary

**What it is**  
Three Word export flavors in the header — all stay DRAFT-stamped. There is no clean non-DRAFT export. Chrome says **Your draft**; Word files still stamp **WORKING COPY** (live `DRAFT / WORKING COPY`) — teach both strings so the stamp is not a surprise. APD hand-off packages remain DRAFT until authentication.

**When to use which**
| Button | Use when | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| **Plain** | Internal read of the full working copy | Full draft Word; live stamp `DRAFT / WORKING COPY` |
| **Track Changes** | WG review that needs change markup | Revision-marked working draft; cover line: `change-markup working draft for WG review` |
| **Summary** | Deltas-only handoff | Summary of Change table only — not the same as browsing Outline Summary of Change; title notes not authenticated under AR 25-30 / DA Pam 25-40 |

**How**
1. Click **Plain**, **Track Changes**, or **Summary** in the top bar (gold export buttons, next to **User Guide** — separate from Training controls).
2. Open the file far enough to confirm stamps always on:
   - Live: header `DRAFT / WORKING COPY` · footer `DRAFT`
   - Training: header `TRAINING / DRAFT / WORKING COPY` · footer `TRAINING / DRAFT`
3. Summary title stays marked *Summary of Change (DRAFT — working copy; not authenticated under AR 25-30 / DA Pam 25-40)*.

**What success looks like**  
The downloaded file clearly reads as draft, and the flavor matches the button you used (full plain vs Track Changes markup vs deltas-only Summary). Seals in the app still sit with the plain gold DRAFT mark.

**Common mistake**  
Treating all three as one generic “Word export”; confusing header **Summary** with the Outline **Summary of Change** browse view; treating DRAFT / WORKING COPY stamps as a bug because chrome said Your draft.

## Part H — Roles, gates, and session safety

### 18. Roles

**What it is**  
Header **Role** gates who can edit, reshape, upload, unlock, or mark WG-ready.

**What you can do**

| Role | You can… |
|---|---|
| **Editor** | Write the draft; reshape Outline; Undo / Save; Tasks; checkpoints; Keep wording / Insert See cite; uploads |
| **Reviewer** | Read and browse everything (including Writing Assistant); cannot change the draft, upload, reset Training, or Unlock |
| **Approver** | Read-only draft text; upload to compare; **Mark this paragraph** / **Mark ready for WG review**; Unlock; reset Training |

**Common mistake**  
A Reviewer expecting Unlock, upload, or Reset to original.

---

### 19. Idle lock

**What it is**  
After about 14 minutes idle you get **IDLE WARNING** / **Session idle for 14 minutes** with **Continue working**. At 15 minutes the current section is saved and the workspace locks (`Idle 15 minutes — saved and locked`). The original regulation is never modified.

**How**
1. Click **Continue working** if you are still editing.
2. If locked: `LOCKED — … Your draft was saved. The original regulation is unchanged.` → **Unlock** (Editor or Approver only).

**What success looks like**  
Draft saved on lock; after Unlock you continue; original unchanged.

**Common mistake**  
Thinking a lock means data loss. Lock saves first.
